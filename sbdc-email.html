The $500 rule

If a newsletter isn’t worth $500 to a client, don’t send it.

The inbox is a privilege. Every send competes with their accountant, their kid’s school, and their best customer — it has to earn the open.

Before you send: does this save them time, make them money, unlock capital, or open a door?

  • Saves time

Your Business People.
Why · Email How-Tos

One clear call to action per send. Every email has one primary goal — schedule a session, register, apply, click through. Don’t split attention across five asks.

Mobile-first, always. Over 60% of NorCal SBDC emails are opened on mobile. Single-column layouts, large tap targets, concise copy, and pre-headers that tell the story before the body loads.

Every client and partner-facing mass email carries the SBA and ADA disclaimers. No exceptions — not for events, not for newsletters, not for one-off announcements.

Welcome drip first. Newsletters second. Promotional sends last. The client journey runs in that order — earn trust through the welcome sequence before you ask for anything.

Subject line is half the email. Lead with the value, not the brand. “Capital access is part of the deal” beats “April newsletter from NorCal SBDC” every time.

Use the standard signature on every send. Logo, gradient rule, contact stack, Berry tagline. Don’t reinvent it per advisor.
